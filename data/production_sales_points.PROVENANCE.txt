production_sales_points.csv  -  PROVENANCE
===========================================

What it is
  Monthly production volumes for 58 production sales points (sales meters)
  belonging to one Texas operator, January 1993 to April 2026, in long format
  (one row per sales point per month). Sales-point names are replaced by codes
  SP01 to SP58; the code-to-name key is held privately by the group.

Source
  Railroad Commission of Texas (RRC), Production Data Query (public record),
  well/lease-level monthly production reports. Retrieved in 2026 and
  aggregated by the group to sales-point level.

Columns
  date             first day of the production month (YYYY-MM-DD)
  sales_point      coded sales meter, SP01 to SP58
  gas_mcf          gas produced in the month, thousand cubic feet (MCF);
                   gas-well gas plus casinghead gas from oil leases
  oil_bbl          liquids produced in the month, barrels: crude oil from
                   oil leases plus condensate from gas wells, reported
                   together as oil
  producing_wells  number of wells (for oil sales points: leases) that reported
                   a non-zero volume that month

Companion file
  production_sales_points_summary.csv: one row per sales point with first month,
  last month with any production, status ("active" = produced between
  January and April 2026, otherwise "inactive"), number of months and the
  maximum producing_wells. 37 sales points are active and 21 are inactive.

How it was built
  1. Each RRC workbook holds one sheet per well plus a "Total" sheet. The
     well sheets were summed for each month; gas-well gas and casinghead gas
     both go into gas_mcf, and condensate and crude oil both go into oil_bbl.
     The RRC records gas-well liquids as condensate and oil-lease liquids as
     oil by well classification; the split was not verified, so the two are
     combined.
  2. An oil lease is reported to the RRC as one lease total, so the separate
     well sheets of one lease repeat the same numbers. Identical sheets were
     counted once (this affects SP11).
  3. "NO RPT" in the RRC data means the well filed no report that month; it is
     counted as zero volume and not as a producing well.
  4. Each sales point starts at its first month with production (or January
     1993, where the RRC download begins) and ends in April 2026.
     April 2026 is the last month with complete filing: from January 2025 to
     April 2026 exactly the same 33 well records show "NO RPT" every month
     (wells permanently off line), but the count rises to 46 in May, 47 in
     June and 122 in July 2026 as operators' reports were still arriving.
     Every month in this file is therefore fully reported.
  5. Check: the summed gas matches the workbook "Total" sheets exactly for every
     sales point. (In the six oil-lease workbooks the Total sheet labels its
     oil and gas columns the wrong way round; the values themselves match.)

Things to know before modelling
  - Volumes are monthly totals, so February is low partly because it has fewer
    days. Divide by the number of days in the month for a daily rate.
  - Zeros are filed reports: real shut-ins, workovers or no-sales months.
  - Jumps in producing_wells mark new wells, recompletions or wells going
    off line: these are structural breaks in a sales point's series.
  - Volumes are gross (8/8ths) production, not the operator's net share, and
    carry no prices or costs.

Load (pandas)
  import pandas as pd
  df = pd.read_csv('data/production_sales_points.csv', parse_dates=['date'])
  gas = df.pivot(index='date', columns='sales_point', values='gas_mcf')   # one column per sales point
